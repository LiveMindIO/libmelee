### Interpretation limits

N1: “while the aerial sweetspot does not deal more damage, it does launch
opponents at a more favorable angle (80° → 84°).” The catalog has exactly one
84-degree zone: ID1/bone15, air-only. ID0's12%/110-degree ground-only sweetspot
does not override this evidence. IDs2/3 hit both target classes at80 degrees.
This report does not append a replacement sweet label for ID1.

N2: “Up aerials are easier to sweetspot, but are weaker.” This occurs in the
comparison with Smash64 Fox, not an exact Falco second-hit table annotation.
No clone comparison or reverse inference from the named body sourspot is used.
All three second-hit zones deal10%; growth120 versus20 alone is not semantic
label evidence. The8–9 opener remains a separate, unadjudicated strike.

N3: “Falcon Punch's sweetspot deals more damage (26% → 27%), takes priority over
the sourspots (ID# 2 → 0) and has a larger hitbox than the mid hit”. Its next
numeric statement explicitly calls23% sour. Only that explicit23% role is used.
The priority sentence is **not** certified as an engine result by this report.
S4 independently lists ground and aerial23% rows and52–56 timing; its overview
says “When it is used in the air, all hitboxes deal slightly higher knockback”.
The unchanged catalog records base30 versus40 and growth102 for those rows.
Both23% zones target grounded and aerial opponents. S4's `effect=Normal`
conflicts with the preserved catalog element1 and flaming-punch prose. This
known discrepancy is disclosed, not repaired or silently used to alter a phase.
